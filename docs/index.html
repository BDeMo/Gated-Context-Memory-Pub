Method

We design compression to preserve useful information and make failures easier to detect. The writer and reader have distinct roles, coordinated through reader-aligned training.

Write informative states

A lightweight writer collects query-conditioned intermediate representations. Its continuous states retain structure without requiring a human-readable summary.

Read—and detect failure

Task, distillation, and reconstruction objectives train the reader to use these states. Its first-token confidence supplies the detection signal, without another predictor.

Detection does not prescribe a correction. An agent harness can retrieve evidence, revise memory, or use raw-context fallback.

The experimental argument

Compact memory.
Transferable detection.

Compression quality, failure detection, and modular transfer—tested across tasks and language-model readers.

01

Compression quality · Main comparison

Does compression remain useful?

On Qwen3-8B, GCM w/o gating leads the reported compact-only baselines across tool use, multi-hop QA, and long-document QA. Gating recovers much of the raw-context performance while retaining the benefits of compressed information.

02

Long-context transfer

Can a source-trained interface handle new contexts?

We train on source-task HotpotQA, freeze the interface, and evaluate on LB-HotpotQA and LB-2WikiMQA without target-specific tuning. This tests longer-context and cross-task transfer.

Source-task HotpotQA and LB-HotpotQA use different samples and protocols, with 4,096- and 16,384-token raw-context windows. Archived LongBench answer extraction can score a reasoning marker instead of the answer, affecting task scores and score-derived detection labels. These results do not isolate compression quality.

03

Failure detection across readers

Does the signal survive a change of backbone?

With separately trained interfaces, source-task HotpotQA detection is useful across five readers. Gating improves over raw context by 0.95–6.16 F1 points. This tests detection across models, not a fixed compressor transplanted between backbones.

04

An alternative writer

Detection is not limited to our compressor.

Replacing the GCM writer with a matched-budget Cartridge implementation tests modular writing and detection. Reader-side confidence remains useful with both writers, with task-dependent differences.

05

Detection and correction

Evaluate the signal, not just the threshold.

In controlled runs, Qwen3.5-9B reaches AUROC 0.84 ± 0.01 on BFCL, while both tested readers are near 0.59 on HotpotQA. Fallback illustrates one use of the signal; another harness can choose its own threshold or correction strategy.

The takeaway

Preservation and detection
are complementary.

Cross-task, cross-reader, and alternative-writer results motivate the hypothesis that compression failures share detectable patterns. GCM studies how to preserve useful information while making its loss visible to the reader.
